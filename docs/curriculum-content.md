### Start At Zero And Count Intervals

**Objective:** identify the starting graduation and count equal spaces from it, keeping the inch unit attached to the value. The audit explicitly connects ruler subdivisions to G10 and starting-graduation alignment to G16; G07 supports the unit.

**Learn:** distinguish the ruler's physical edge from zero. On a scale divided into eighths, demonstrate that the first interval ends at 1/8 inch and three intervals end at 3/8 inch. Let the student move a marker and show/hide the zero numeral while leaving its graduation identifiable.

**Challenge:** with the ruler's edge inset from zero, request 3/8 inch and ask where counting began. Accept the 3/8-inch position relative to zero under the reviewed hit tolerance; the explanation must identify the zero graduation, not the physical edge. Give feedback about starting reference or counting spaces when relevant. A virtual choice is not proof of physical ruler-placement skill.

### Read Fractional Inches

**Objective:** locate fractional-inch positions through sixteenths. The audit's G11 is directly based on the inch ruler sequence; G10 supplies equal-interval meaning.

**Learn:** review halves, quarters, eighths, and sixteenths with consistent one-inch lengths. Work through 5/8 inch as five eighth-inch intervals, also locating it at 10/16 on a sixteenth-inch ruler. Let learners revisit larger subdivisions instead of locking them into a linear progression.

**Challenge:** request 7/16 inch on a sixteenth-inch scale without answer highlighting. Accept the seventh interval endpoint after zero, using the same nonoverlapping tolerance for every target. Feedback can prompt checking the denominator or counting intervals; it should not diagnose the cause of every wrong click with certainty.

### Equivalent Fractions Share A Position

**Objective:** recognize that different fractional names can identify the same length. G12 in the audit ties equivalence to common ruler locations.

**Learn:** demonstrate 1/2, 2/4, 4/8, and 8/16 at one position on the same one-inch whole. Change the displayed partition without changing the ruler's unit length. Have learners predict the shared location before revealing it.

**Challenge:** ask for the position of 6/8 inch, then ask whether 3/4 inch names that same length and why. The position is 12/16 inch; an acceptable explanation uses equal ruler locations or equal subdivision counts. Score the location and equivalence explanation separately. Internal normalization alone is not student evidence. A follow-up comparison does not require cutting a second piece of the same length.

### Whole Inches And A Fraction

**Objective:** combine a whole-inch amount with its fractional remainder. The audit explicitly identifies this skill as G13.

**Learn:** show 2 1/4 inches as two complete inches followed by one quarter-inch interval. Keep the fractional part anchored after the second inch, and contrast it with 1/4 inch from zero.

**Challenge:** request 1 5/8 inches. Accept 26/16 inch from zero; preserve a response at 5/8 inch as a different location and suggest checking the whole-inch part. The worked answer is 16/16 + 10/16 = 26/16 inch. Do not make arithmetic beyond the reviewed learning target an unrelated barrier to selecting the ruler position.

## Extensions And Free Exploration

Decimal-inch representations and sparse-scale empty-space selections remain proposed local extensions. Record their mathematical rationale and source relationship explicitly before adding Learn lessons or Challenge checks; do not relabel them as direct PLTW requirements. See [Ruler Interaction](ruler-interaction.md). The core Learn and Challenge content above stays linked to the audited fractional-inch goals.

Free can expose ruler subdivisions, equivalent displays, chosen lengths, cuts, and duplication without prescribed prompts. It may support these same ideas, but its explorations do not generate passed comprehension results. Model assembly and machine animation remain context rather than evidence of physical construction goals.

## Required Record For Every Lesson And Challenge

Each authored entry must retain its stable game content ID and include:

- A plain-language learning objective and links to the class repository, relevant item, and exact planning-document revision/section.
- Qualified goal IDs, source-page evidence, and whether the relationship is direct, inferred, or a local extension; explain why the activity measures or practices that goal.
- The student action, exact prompt, worked explanation/answer, unit, ruler assumptions, and accepted answer or tolerance.
- Available scaffolds, hints, accessibility controls, feedback, and likely misconceptions.
- For Learn, a complete fundamental review plus guided practice and direct menu navigation.
- For Challenge, the observable evidence, scoring/interpretation rule, treatment of retries/assistance, and linked Learn review.
- Content revision and review status; links to its actual implementation and teacher guide when those exist.

Use these records to keep the planned in-game **Curriculum** page and **What This Practices** links current. A release must not contain an unindexed Learn lesson or Challenge task. Review full authored content and the relevant primary-source pages before treating these draft entries as classroom-ready materials.
